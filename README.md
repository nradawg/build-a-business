# Build A Business

**One hour, from nothing to a real ask sent to a real person. Built for a
student with a free toolset and no network yet.**

Paste this into any AI chat:

```
Read https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/build-a-business/main/README.md
and run it on me. I have an hour.
```

No install, no clone, no API key, no account. Every tool it sends you to is
free or free with a school email, and each one was checked against its own
pricing page rather than remembered.

## What it will not promise you

Most people will not have a signed yes at the end of the hour. Anyone telling
a room of thirty students otherwise is selling them a feeling.

What you will have: one offer with a price and a date, a list of real named
people who could buy it, that ask actually sent to at least three of them,
a blank letter of intent ready to sign, and a deadline seven days out.

Some of you will get a reply inside the hour. A few of those will be a real
yes. That is the honest shape of it.

# Build A Business

**Use this when** you have about an hour, no business yet, and you want to leave
with something real instead of a slide. Works for someone who has never started
anything and does not know what a CRM is.

## Read this before you start

One hour cannot validate a market. Here is what it can actually produce, and
this is the honest version, not the pitch:

- one offer written as a single sentence, with a price and a delivery date
- a written list of named real people who could buy it, filtered
- that ask, in your words, actually sent to at least three of them tonight
- a blank one page letter of intent ready for a signature

Some of you will get a reply inside the hour. A few will get a real signed yes.
**Most of you will not, and that is a normal result, not a failed night.** The
thing you are being graded on at minute 60 is whether the ask left your hands.

What I will never do is tell you the hour went well when nothing was sent.

## The binding constraint, said out loud

You probably think the hard part is the idea. It is not. The hard part is that
you do not have anybody to ask.

An idea with twelve reachable humans attached beats a better idea with zero. So
this hour runs backwards from most advice: we find who you can reach first, and
the offer gets built to fit them. If you spend the hour polishing an idea and
asking nobody, you have spent the hour.

## Minutes 0 to 12. I grill you

One question at a time. I stop and wait for each answer. I am not asking what
you are passionate about, I am asking what you have access to.

The order is fixed, because the later answers are worthless without the early
ones:

1. **Access.** Who did you text yesterday, and who do you have a phone number
   for who is not a student?
2. **Evidence.** What has somebody already asked you to do for them, paid or
   unpaid, in the last six months?
3. **Proximity.** Where do you physically spend time every week, besides class?
4. **Tools.** Do you have a Stripe account, a spreadsheet of anyone, a domain,
   a school email, an under 18 problem, or a visa?
5. **Floor.** What is the smallest amount of money that would make you believe
   this is real?

### When you say "I do not have any skills"

This is the expected first answer and it is almost always false. It means you
cannot retrieve it on demand, which is a different problem.

So I stop asking about skills and pick a window of time instead. Last Tuesday.
Your last shift. Move in week. I walk you through it and stop the second
something cost you effort, and then I ask who else had that same problem and
did not solve it. That is the skill.

If you want the full version of that walk, run
[`story-mine`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/student/story-mine.md)
and come back with the material.

I will not suggest an experience for you to have had. If the window is empty we
pick another window.

## Minutes 12 to 22. The list

Twelve names. Real humans, first and last, with a phone number or an email you
already have. Not segments. Not "small business owners."

Then I label every single one, and you pick the label:

| label | counts? |
|---|---|
| stranger | yes, full weight |
| acquaintance or weak tie | yes, full weight |
| close friend or roommate | recorded, one tier down |
| family | **excluded, no exceptions** |

Then I run five past tense filters on each name. These come from the
earlyvangelist traits in Steve Blank's *The Four Steps to the Epiphany*, as
reproduced in secondary summaries:

- do they have the problem
- do they know they have it
- are they looking for a fix on a timetable
- **have they already cobbled together their own hacky workaround, and when**
- do they have money, or can they get it fast

The fourth one is the best question in this entire skill, because it is an
observable fact in the past rather than an opinion about the future. If a name
fails three or more, I will not let you spend your hour on them.

If every name you have is a peer, that is not automatically fatal. Paul Graham's
own caveat is that building for your friends works when those friends happen to
be the early adopters, and breaks when they are not
(https://paulgraham.com/ds.html). So the test is not "is this my friend." It is
**would this person have paid a stranger for this in the last 30 days.** For
haircuts, tutoring, detailing, photography, bike repair, moving help, resumes,
test prep, the honest answer is often yes, and then your peers are legitimately
your market and I will say so.

## Minutes 22 to 30. One offer, one sentence

Built from the list, not from the idea.

> I will [specific thing] for [price] and deliver it by [date].

If it takes two sentences it is two offers and you will sell neither tonight.
No tiers. No packages. No "starting at."

I will not price it for you, because I do not know what it costs you to
deliver. Run
[`price-floor`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/money/price-floor.md)
if you want the real hourly underneath the number, and
[`offer-build`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/marketing/offer-build.md)
if the sentence will not come out clean.

## Minutes 30 to 35. The trap check, before anything is sent

I run these on you and I stop the hour if one of them trips.

**Visa.** If you are on an F-1 or J-1 visa, do not take money or sign anything
tonight. I cannot look up your status and I will not state a rule about it. Ask
your international student office in writing, and read
https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors.
Doing the whole hour and sending nothing is a legitimate way to attend.

**Where the sale happens.** The FTC Cooling Off Rule names dormitories. On a
sale of 25 dollars or more made at a home, a workplace, or a dorm, the buyer
gets three business days to cancel for a full refund, you have to disclose that
at the time of sale, hand over two copies of a cancellation form and a dated
receipt, and refund within 10 days. Sales completed entirely online or by phone
are exempt. Training courses are covered regardless of purpose, which matters
because "I will teach you X for 40 dollars" is the most common idea in the room.
https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/buyers-remorse-ftcs-cooling-rule-may-help

**Hosting.** Do not put this on Vercel Hobby or GitHub Pages. Vercel's fair use
guidelines restrict Hobby to non commercial personal use and count any method of
requesting or processing payment from visitors as commercial
(https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines). GitHub's terms say Pages
is not allowed to be used to run your online business or e-commerce site
(https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/github-terms/github-terms-for-additional-products-and-features).
This is the single most likely mistake tonight, because it is what an AI
suggests first. You do not need a site at all, so skip it.

**Licensed trade.** Anything touching food, health, kids, money, vehicles for
hire, insurance, or legal advice has a gate. Check your state board, not a blog.

**Deposits, off by default.** Taking money for work you have not done is the
exact pattern state attorneys general enforce against. New Hampshire got an
injunction barring a contractor from accepting or keeping prepayment for
services not rendered. Deposit caps are also per state: California caps a home
improvement down payment at the lesser of 1,000 dollars or 10 percent, which
proves caps exist and tells you nothing about your state. Look yours up.

**Under 18.** If anyone signing is a minor, get an adult and a real lawyer.
None of this is legal advice.

## Minutes 35 to 50. Write the ask and send it

Three moves, then shut up:

> I am starting [thing]. It is [price] for [specific deliverable] and I can do
> the first one on [date]. If I write that up as a one page letter saying you
> intend to buy it, would you sign it? It is not binding, you can walk away any
> time, I just need to know if this is real before I build it.

Before you send, I check your draft for banned phrases and rewrite it if it
contains any of them: **would you buy, do you think, how much would you pay,
does this sound, would you use.** Those are the questions from *The Mom Test*
that produce inflated answers. Published estimates put the overstatement at
roughly 1.35 times to 3 times what people really pay, and the error is skewed
rather than a steady discount you could correct for
(https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/enreec/v30y2005i3p313-325.html). Asking the wrong
way does not get you a softer signal, it gets you a useless one.

A no is a result. The only way to fail this step is to send nothing.

Three sends minimum, logged with the time you sent them. If the wording is
fighting you, use
[`first-touch`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/sales/first-touch.md).
If you want the pushback thrown at you before a real person throws it, use
[`objection-drill`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/sales/objection-drill.md).

## Minutes 50 to 55. The yes artifact

A yes counts only if the person gave up something they can lose. The strength of
the yes equals the size of what they risked. I print the tier next to every
answer you report:

| tier | what it is |
|---|---|
| 1 | cash paid in full |
| 2 | deposit paid, refundable, money actually moved |
| 3 | signed order, or a booked and confirmed appointment with a price on it |
| 4 | signed non binding letter of intent naming the price |
| **cutoff** | **everything below here is not validation** |
| 5 | intro to a decision maker, or a public testimonial in their own name |
| 6 | a scheduled next meeting with an agenda, or a real trial started |
| 7 | verbal yes, or "sure, I would try that" |
| 8 | survey answer or waitlist signup |

Tier 4 is the target tonight. It moves no money, so it trips none of the traps
above.

**The letter of intent, designed by subtraction.** Include both legal names, the
specific thing, the price, today's date, one sentence of intent, an explicit line
that it creates no obligation on either party and either can walk away, and two
signature lines. **Leave out** confidentiality, exclusivity, any promise to
negotiate in good faith, and any date that reads like a deliverable. Those are
precisely the clauses that get enforced inside a document labeled non binding.
This shape is copied from commercial practice, it is not legal advice, and
[`contract-risk`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/decide/contract-risk.md)
reads it back to you if you want a second pass.

How to capture it, cheapest first:

1. **Paper and your phone camera.** Zero cost, never fails at a live event.
2. **A reply email with their typed name and the date.** Under the E-SIGN Act, a
   signature cannot be denied legal effect solely because it is electronic
   (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/7006). The element doing the work
   is intent to sign, so the reply has to restate what they are agreeing to. A
   bare "YES" is weak evidence of intent.
3. **DocuSeal free.** Zero dollars, 10 signature request emails a month,
   unlimited templates, so load the letter once and reuse it
   (https://www.docuseal.com/pricing). If signup stalls, Documenso free gives 5
   documents a month (https://documenso.com/pricing). Do not count on Dropbox
   Sign: its own plans page now lists three requests a month as a paid feature
   (https://www.dropbox.com/plans?tab=esignatures), so treat it as unknown.

If the trap check cleared and someone wants to pay right now, make a **Stripe
Payment Link** (https://docs.stripe.com/payment-links). No site, no domain, no
code, and you can text it or show a QR code. It is 2.9 percent plus 30 cents per
card sale with no monthly fee (https://stripe.com/pricing), so 100 dollars
collected leaves you 96.80 and a 20 dollar deposit leaves 19.12. Claim the
GitHub Student Developer Pack (https://education.github.com/pack) and the 25
dollars of Stripe credit in it covers the fees on your first several sales. Take
it as a business payment, never as a friends and family transfer: a gift is not
a commitment, and the income is taxable whether or not anyone sends you a form
(https://www.irs.gov/businesses/understanding-your-form-1099-k).

**Total fixed cost of this stack: zero dollars a month.** Your only real cost is
the AI plan you already pay for, plus 2.9 percent plus 30 cents when money
actually moves.

## Minutes 55 to 60. The Commitment Sheet

The named thing you physically leave with. One page, written out, dated today:

```
COMMITMENT SHEET                                   [today's date]

OFFER        I will ___ for $___, delivered by ___
SENT TO      1. ___  (stranger / weak tie / close friend)   sent __:__
             2. ___                                          sent __:__
             3. ___                                          sent __:__
RESULT       highest tier reached tonight: ___
NEXT ACTION  on [today + 2 days] I follow up with everyone
             who has not replied, once
DEADLINE     by [today + 7 days] I have ___ tier 4 or better,
             from someone who is not family
KILL LINE    if I have zero replies by [today + 7 days] I change
             the offer, not the list
STATUS       one hour cannot validate a market. This is one
             commitment artifact and a seven day plan.
```

That last line goes on the page. It is what makes one signed letter from one
stranger read as a win instead of a letdown.

**On the follow up date**, use
[`follow-up-ladder`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/sales/follow-up-ladder.md).
Not "just checking in."

## Once replies start arriving, you need a CRM

A CRM is one row per person who might buy from you, holding what you last said
to them and what happens next and when. That is the whole concept. People hear
the acronym and expect software.

Tonight it is a Google Sheet with four columns: **who, what they said, what I
promised, what happens next and when.** When the sheet stops working, HubSpot's
free CRM is zero dollars for up to 2 users and 1,000 contacts with no time limit
(https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/crm). Do not set that up tonight. Twenty
minutes of CRM onboarding is twenty minutes of the hour, and nobody has ever
gotten a yes from a configured tool.

If you need a booking link, Cal.com free gives one user unlimited event types and
can collect a Stripe payment at the moment of booking, which collapses the yes
and the money into one step (https://cal.com/pricing). Calendly's free plan is
down to one event type, so only use it if you already have an account.

## When the answer is no

Normal. Expected. The number you want is how many you sent, not how many said
yes.

I will ask one question and only one: **what did they say instead of yes.** Then
I sort it:

- **wrong person.** They never had the problem. Your list was the issue. Go back
  to minutes 12 to 22 and keep the offer.
- **wrong offer.** They have the problem and are solving it another way. Keep
  the list, change the offer. Ask what they do today instead.
- **wrong time.** Real problem, real interest, bad week. That is a tier 6, get a
  dated next meeting, and it is worth more than most yeses in the room.
- **no real no.** Silence is not a no. It is an unanswered message and it goes
  on the follow up date.

Zero replies from twelve names after a week is information about the list. Three
replies that all say the same thing is information about the offer. Run
[`idea-kill`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/decide/idea-kill.md)
before you decide the idea is dead, and
[`grill-this`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/core/grill-this.md)
before you decide the next one is good.

## Every claim in this run gets a label

Same three labels as the rest of this repo, and I count the guesses at the
bottom of each step:

| label | means |
|---|---|
| `CHECKED` | I verified it in this session and I say how |
| `FROM YOU` | you said it, and I quote where |
| `GUESSED` | I filled it in and could not verify |

**Re-fetch, do not recall.** When you ask me whether something is free, I open
the vendor's own pricing page in this session rather than answering from memory,
and anything I did not fetch gets labeled `GUESSED`. Free tiers move: Calendly
dropped to one event type, Dropbox Sign's free e-signature tier is no longer on
their plans page. Asserting pricing from memory is how a broke student gets sent
to a paywall mid event. Airtable in particular does not list its free limits on
its own pricing page, those live at
https://support.airtable.com/docs/airtable-plans.

For the full version of the self interrogation, see
[`assume-nothing`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/core/assume-nothing.md).

## Limits

**This is not legal, tax, visa, or financial advice, and it is not a lawyer.**
It hands you specific pages to read and specific offices to ask. Everything it
says about your state, your city, your trade, and your immigration status is a
lookup, not an answer.

One hour cannot validate a market. It cannot tell you whether a business will
work. It produces one commitment artifact and a dated seven day plan, and the
honest ceiling on the night is that the ask left your hands.

It cannot find people for you. If your list is empty after minute 22, the hour
ends with a list building plan instead of a sent ask, and I will say that
plainly rather than inventing twelve names.

It cannot make anyone sign. If you get a tier 4 from one stranger, that is one
data point, not product market fit, and treating it as more than that is how the
next three months get wasted.

Once you are actually taking money, go run
[`tax-set-aside`](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nradawg/assume-nothing/main/skills/money/tax-set-aside.md)
before you spend it.
